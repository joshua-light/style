Completing the square is a move you can watch

The quadratic formula looks like a spell. It is the record of three moves on a picture: cut a strip, swing one half, fill a corner. The films below make each move in front of you.

For anyone who met the formula before the picture · about 4 minutes · 3 films

A puzzle from the year 820

Around 820, in Baghdad, al-Khwarizmi wrote the book that gave algebra its name. One of its problems:

What must be the square which, when increased by ten of its own roots, amounts to thirty-nine?

Al-Khwarizmi, about 820, in Frederic Rosen's translation of 1831

How to watch. Play shows one beat and stops. Press it again for the next. Replay repeats a beat. Drag the bar to scrub, backwards too.

A root is the side of the square. Today we write the puzzle as x2 + 10x = 39. Al-Khwarizmi had no symbols. He drew it.

Start by drawing each term. A dashed box is a part not drawn yet.

The colour of a term is the colour of its shape, so a piece can be followed from the equation into the picture. Hover one to light both.

Three moves

A lone square is easy to solve: if x2 = 49, the side is 7. The strip is what makes the puzzle hard. So reshape the strip until the whole figure is a square again.

The film stops before the third move and asks you to predict it.

Predict

The film holds at the missing corner. With a strip 10 wide, how much area does the corner need?

Each half of the strip is 5 wide, so the gap is 5 by 5: 25. Half the strip's width, squared. The film goes on.

Nothing in the moves depends on 10 and 39. Set the two sliders under the film to other numbers and play it again: the same cut, swing, and fill solve every puzzle of this kind.

What the picture cannot show

A length cannot be negative, so the picture finds one answer. The equation has two.

Read the graph from its lowest point. The answers sit 8 to either side of -5: 3 and -13. The -5 is half the strip, turned negative. The 8 is the side of the completed square.

The formula is the film, written down

Put letters where the numbers were: a strip b wide and a total of c. Every part of the formula is one beat.

x=±√(c + (b / 2)2)-b / 2

  1. b / 2. Cut the strip in half.
  2. (b / 2)2. The corner the two halves leave open.
  3. c + (b / 2)2. Fill it, and the whole square has this area.
  4. The root. The side of that square.
  5. Minus b / 2. Take the half strip off the side, and x is left.
  6. Plus or minus. The curve reaches the total twice.
